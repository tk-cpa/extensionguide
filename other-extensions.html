An extension to file is not an extension to pay tax.

This page collects the extension rules for tax types that do not fit neatly into the individual, business, estate, sales, or excise categories covered elsewhere on this site: federal payroll (employment) tax, and FBAR foreign account reporting. Both were checked directly against current primary sources this session.

Federal payroll (employment) tax - Forms 941 and 940

There is no extension of time to file Form 941 (Employer's Quarterly Federal Tax Return) or Form 940 (Employer's Annual Federal Unemployment (FUTA) Tax Return). Neither form appears on the Form 7004 eligible-forms list, and neither form's own instructions provide a request-based extension mechanism.

What does exist is a narrower, automatic accommodation for compliant filers: if all employment taxes were deposited on time for the period, the employer gets 10 additional calendar days to file the return itself. This is not an extension request - there is nothing to apply for - and it does not apply to the deposits themselves, only to the paper or e-filed return, and only when every deposit for that period was already made on time.

State unemployment insurance quarterly reports

State unemployment insurance (SUI/SUTA) quarterly wage and tax reports are filed with each state's workforce or labor agency, not its revenue department, on a fixed quarterly cycle (typically the last day of the month following the quarter's end). All 50 states plus DC are addressed in the table below, each resolved with a definitive, cited answer, mostly against statutes and administrative regulations rather than agency web pages once earlier web-page checks proved inconclusive.

Honest scope note. All 50 states plus DC are addressed in the table above, every one with a definitive, cited answer - reached, where an agency web page did not resolve the question, by going to the underlying statute or administrative regulation instead. Six confirmed exceptions were found: Arkansas (up to 30 days, good cause), Idaho (up to 60 days, good cause), Missouri (up to 3 months, good cause), New Hampshire (30 days, on timely application and a $50 fee), New Mexico (up to 30 days, good cause), and Washington (good cause, with a deposit requirement). Three narrow, non-general exceptions were found: Connecticut (15 extra days, but only for reimbursing employers), and Tennessee and South Carolina (each a payment extension only, not a filing extension). The remaining states with a definitive answer show no general extension mechanism (Michigan's and North Carolina's accommodations were one-time or disaster-specific, not standing rights). New Mexico's own dws.nm.gov domain rejected every direct fetch attempted this session; its answer was reached instead through the regulation's independent publication on the state's official register archive and cross-checked against a second independent secondary source - the citation is to the regulation itself, not to the agency's own page.

FBAR - FinCEN Form 114 (Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts)

FBAR is not a tax return - it is an information report filed with the Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, separate from any IRS income tax filing - but it carries its own automatic extension that is worth stating plainly since it is easy to confuse with the Form 4868 individual extension.

ItemDetail
Original due dateApril 15
Extended due dateOctober 15, automatically
Is a request required?No - FinCEN grants the extension to every filer automatically; there is no FBAR-specific extension form
AuthorityFinCEN, Due Date for FBARs guidance
The FBAR automatic extension to October 15 runs on its own, separate from Form 4868. A filer does not need to have requested the federal income tax extension for the FBAR extension to apply.
